By the second week of elimination, most people have worked out that breakfast is the meal the diet hits hardest. Cereal is out, most breads are out, and the savoury options that survive — eggs, essentially — get boring fast when the only thing you are allowed to do with them is scramble them plain.

The problem is not the eggs. It is that every technique you would normally reach for to make eggs interesting starts with onion or garlic.

## Where the flavour comes from instead

Three things carry this dish, and none of them is allium.

The first is the garlic-infused oil, which does the aromatic work in the first minute of cooking. The second is the tomato, cooked hard enough to break down and concentrate rather than just warmed through — that is where the savoury depth comes from, and it is why the recipe asks you to press the tomatoes with a spoon rather than leave them in neat pieces. The third is the feta, which is doing the job salt alone cannot: it adds sharpness and a fermented tang that reads as complexity.

## The spinach arithmetic

Spinach is the ingredient people get wrong here, and always in the same direction.

Baby spinach is low FODMAP at 75 g per serving, which sounds enormous when you are holding raw leaves and looks like nothing once it has wilted. A 150 g bag — the standard supermarket size — is exactly two servings. The recipe is built around that bag deliberately, so the arithmetic is done for you as long as you split the pan between two people.

If you cook the whole bag and eat the whole pan, you have had a double serving of spinach, roughly 80 g of feta and two tomatoes in one sitting. Individually all three are fine. Stacked, they are a plausible reason to have a bad afternoon and no obvious culprit.

## On the toast

Bread is the other place this meal can quietly go wrong. Ordinary wheat bread is high in fructans and out during elimination. Two options work: a certified gluten-free loaf, or genuine spelt sourdough, which is low FODMAP at two slices because the long fermentation breaks down most of the fructans before you ever eat it.

The word doing the work there is *genuine*. A great deal of supermarket "sourdough" is ordinary yeasted bread with added acidity for flavour, produced on a timescale that ferments nothing. If the ingredients list yeast, it is not going to help you.
