Ask a room of people three weeks into elimination what they miss most and the answer is rarely something exotic. It is pasta with tomato sauce — the default weeknight dinner, the one that no longer works because every jarred sauce on the shelf lists onion and garlic in its first four ingredients.

The fix is straightforward once you stop trying to replace garlic and start replacing what garlic *does*.

## What the infused oil is actually doing

Garlic's contribution to a tomato sauce is an aromatic, faintly sweet savouriness that appears in the first minute of cooking. That compound set is fat-soluble. The fructans that cause your symptoms are water-soluble and do not migrate into oil. Infusing therefore separates the two almost perfectly: you get the flavour, and the fructans stay behind in the discarded solids.

Buy it rather than making it. Home-infusing garlic in oil and storing it creates a low-oxygen environment that can support *Clostridium botulinum*, and this is a genuinely serious risk rather than a cautious footnote. Commercial products are acidified or otherwise stabilised to prevent it.

## The tomato arithmetic

Canned tomatoes are low FODMAP up to 92 g per serving, which is a smaller amount than most people assume — roughly a quarter of a standard can. Splitting one 400 g can across four servings lands just under the line. If you are cooking for two, use half the can, not the whole one, and freeze the rest.
