Breakfast is where the elimination phase tends to break down first. Most standard breakfasts are built on wheat, honey, apple or pear juice, or ordinary milk, and the convenient options in a supermarket chiller cabinet are almost uniformly off-limits. Having one reliable breakfast that you can assemble half-asleep is worth more than a repertoire of ten you never actually make.

## The portion is the recipe

It is tempting to treat oats as unlimited because they are wholesome, and this is the mistake that catches people out. Oats carry both fructans and GOS, and the tested threshold sits at about half a cup dry per sitting. Below that you are fine; at a full cup you have quietly eaten a high-FODMAP meal and will spend the morning wondering what went wrong.

This is the clearest everyday illustration of a principle that runs through the whole protocol: FODMAP content is a dose, not a property. Very few foods are permanently forbidden. Most simply have a ceiling, and the diet becomes far less frightening once you start thinking in thresholds rather than in banned lists.

## Watch the milk

Oat milk is the trap in this particular recipe. It is an excellent low-FODMAP choice in coffee, but pouring it over oats stacks the same FODMAPs twice in one bowl. Lactose-free dairy, almond or macadamia milk all keep the total where you want it.
