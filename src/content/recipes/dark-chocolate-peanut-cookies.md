Most low-FODMAP baking recipes are ordinary recipes with the flour swapped out, and they taste like it. The blends are expensive, they behave differently from what they replaced, and half of them lean on ingredients that create a new problem while solving the old one.

The better move is to cook things that never needed flour.

## Why these hold together

Peanut butter is roughly half fat and a quarter protein. When you beat it with sugar and an egg, the protein sets in the oven and the sugar caramelises — which is structurally most of what flour and butter do in a conventional cookie. Nothing is missing, so nothing needs substituting.

That also makes the recipe unusually hard to get wrong. There is no gluten to overdevelop and no flour-to-liquid ratio to balance.

## Peanuts are the exception among legumes

It is reasonable to be suspicious. Legumes are the classic high-FODMAP category — chickpeas, lentils and dried beans are loaded with galacto-oligosaccharides, and they are among the first things people cut.

Peanuts are botanically legumes but they do not carry a meaningful GOS load. Monash puts peanut butter comfortably in the low-FODMAP range at 50 g, which is more than most people eat in a sitting. Each cookie here holds about 21 g.

The catch is not the peanuts. It is what gets added to them.

## Read the jar

This is the one place this recipe can genuinely go wrong, and it happens at the supermarket rather than in the kitchen.

A growing number of peanut butters — particularly the reduced-fat, "high protein" and "gut friendly" ones — add inulin or chicory root fibre to replace the fat. Inulin *is* fructan. It is the same compound that makes onion and garlic a problem, in a more concentrated form, marketed as a health benefit. Some products also sweeten with honey, which brings excess fructose.

What you want is a jar whose ingredients read: peanuts, salt. Occasionally just peanuts.

## The chocolate ceiling

Dark chocolate is low FODMAP at 30 g. Milk chocolate is lower at 20 g, because the lactose counts, and white chocolate lower again.

At 100 g across twelve cookies, each one carries about 8 grams. Two cookies is a comfortable serving on both counts. It is worth knowing the number rather than guessing, because chocolate is the ingredient people reach for a third of without registering it.
