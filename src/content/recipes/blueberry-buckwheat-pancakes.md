Pancakes look like an obvious casualty of elimination. Wheat flour is out, honey is out, ordinary milk is out at anything above a splash, and most gluten-free flour blends lean on ingredients that are worse than what they replaced.

Buckwheat solves it more elegantly than a blend does, because it is not a substitution at all. It is simply a different, better-tasting ingredient that happens to be safe.

## Buckwheat is not wheat

The name is one of the more unhelpful accidents in food. Buckwheat is not a grass, not a cereal and not related to wheat. It is the seed of a plant closer to rhubarb, it contains no gluten, and it is low FODMAP at 100 g of flour — a threshold you would struggle to reach in a single sitting.

What it gives you is flavour. Wheat flour is close to neutral; buckwheat is nutty, faintly earthy and slightly bitter in the way good rye is. If you have been eating fairly bland food for a month, that is the point.

The trade-off is texture. Buckwheat has no gluten to build structure with, so used alone it bakes dense and a little crumbly. The 3:1 ratio with white rice flour here is what keeps the crumb light without diluting the flavour.

## Twenty berries

Blueberries are the ceiling in this recipe, and the number is lower than most people expect: 40 g per serving, roughly 20 berries. Beyond that the excess fructose adds up.

That is why the ingredient list specifies 160 g across four servings and why the method has you pressing six or seven berries into each pancake rather than folding a punnet through the bowl. Counting them into the pan is not fussiness — it is the only way to know what you actually ate.

## The rest

Two smaller things are worth knowing.

Sugar is fine. Ordinary sucrose contains glucose and fructose in equal measure, and the glucose carries the fructose across the gut wall, so it causes no problem at normal amounts. Honey does not work this way — it has substantially more fructose than glucose, which is exactly the imbalance that triggers symptoms. Maple syrup is safe at 2 tablespoons.

Milk choice matters more than it looks. Lactose-free dairy and almond milk are both comfortable here. Oat milk is only low FODMAP at 125 ml, and this batter uses 300 ml, so it would put you over before you had eaten anything.
