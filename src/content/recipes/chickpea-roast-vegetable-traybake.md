Almost every low-FODMAP recipe you will find, on this site included, is written for the elimination phase. That makes sense — elimination is the hardest part, the part with the most rules, and the part where people most need help.

It also means the recipes quietly stop being useful at exactly the moment the protocol is working.

## This one is not elimination-safe

Stated plainly, because the whole point of the recipe is that it sits on the other side of a line.

Canned chickpeas are low FODMAP at 1/4 cup drained — 42 g, which is about two heaped tablespoons. This dish puts roughly 120 g on each plate. That is a genuine dose of galacto-oligosaccharides, and if you are six weeks into elimination it will probably cause symptoms.

If you have completed a GOS challenge and tolerated it, it will probably not. That is the entire purpose of the reintroduction phase, and this is what the payoff is supposed to look like.

## Why it matters that the payoff exists

The most common way this diet goes wrong is that people finish elimination, feel better, and never reintroduce anything. Restriction is uncomfortable but it is *legible* — you know what to do. Reintroduction means deliberately eating something that might make you feel unwell, on purpose, to gather information.

Given the choice, plenty of people simply stay in elimination. The consequences are real: a needlessly narrow diet, reduced fibre and dietary diversity, measurable changes to the gut microbiome, and for some people a slide into genuinely disordered eating around food.

The long-term goal was never a permanently restricted diet. It was the widest diet your gut will tolerate, which for most people is considerably wider than the elimination phase.

A recipe like this is what that looks like on a plate.

## If you are not there yet

Use one can instead of two.

That brings each serving to about 1/4 cup of chickpeas, inside the elimination threshold, and everything else on the tray is already safe at these quantities — courgette at 65 g per serve, red pepper at 75 g, feta at 40 g, potato and carrot unrestricted. Add 200 g more baby potatoes to make up the bulk and the dish works exactly the same way.

Then come back to the full version when you have earned it.

## Rinsing is worth doing properly

GOS are water-soluble, and a meaningful fraction of what is in a can of chickpeas has leached into the liquid around them.

Draining and then rinsing under running water for a good thirty seconds lowers the dose measurably. It is not a trick that makes a high-GOS portion into a low one, and it should not be treated as one. But it is free, and the difference is not trivial.
