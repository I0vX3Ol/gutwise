James is a careful person. He read the guidance, cleared his cupboards, cooked from scratch most nights and kept a tracker from day one. Four weeks in, his symptom scores had not moved at all.

"I was fairly sure I had done it properly. That was almost the worst part — I had given up bread and apples and beer for a month, I had a spreadsheet, and the line was completely flat. My conclusion was that this was another thing that did not work for me."

## The thing he missed

He mentioned it in passing to his GP, listing what he had been eating. Soup most lunchtimes, he said. Homemade, from scratch.

"She asked what I made the stock with. And I said, well, stock cubes, obviously — I did not make stock from bones like some sort of Victorian. And she said, have you read the ingredients."

Onion powder, second on the list. Garlic powder, fourth.

"It was in the soup, it was in the risotto, it was in the gravy, it was in anything I braised. I had been eating concentrated fructans four or five times a week for a month while believing I was on a strict elimination diet."

## What happened next

He switched to a certified low-FODMAP stock and carried on with everything else unchanged.

"Nine days. That was it. After four weeks of nothing, nine days and the bloating had gone. I felt slightly ridiculous, and mostly just relieved that there had been a reason."

His symptom scores dropped from an average of six to an average of two over the following fortnight, and held there.

## Why he wanted this written up

James asked us to publish this specifically because of how close he came to abandoning the protocol.

"If my GP had not asked that one question, I would have finished week six, told everyone the low-FODMAP diet did nothing for me, and gone back to how things were. And I would have been completely wrong, and I would have believed it for years."

"Nobody warns you hard enough about stock. Everyone tells you about onions and garlic as vegetables. Nobody says: the powdered version is in almost every savoury product you own, listed as natural flavouring, and it will quietly invalidate your entire experiment."

He is midway through reintroduction now — lactose and sorbitol both clear, onion confirmed as a genuine trigger at moderate doses.

"The irony is not lost on me. The thing that broke the elimination phase turned out to be the thing I actually react to. At least the answer was consistent."
