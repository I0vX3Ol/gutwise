Maria had been managing IBS symptoms for six years before she tried the low-FODMAP diet properly. She had attempted a version of it twice before, both times from a printable list found online, and both times she abandoned it after a fortnight.

"The lists never agreed with each other," she says. "One said tomatoes were fine and another said avoid them. I had no idea which to believe, so I just started avoiding everything either one mentioned. That is not a diet, that is a panic."

## What changed the third time

Two things. She bought the Monash app, and she wrote things down.

"The app settled the arguments. There was one source with actual numbers, and portion sizes, and I stopped trying to referee between blog posts. And the tracker meant that at the end of four weeks I had a page of evidence instead of a vague sense that maybe things were better."

Her elimination phase ran four weeks. The improvement was clear by day ten — bloating first, then the pain, and finally a bowel habit she could predict.

## The part she nearly skipped

"I did not want to do reintroduction. I felt well for the first time in years, and the whole thing sounded like deliberately making myself ill for ten weeks. My dietitian was blunt about it. She said if I stopped there I would be avoiding five groups when I probably reacted to one, and I would never know which."

She started with lactose, because milk in coffee was what she missed most.

"Nothing. Full dose, three days, nothing at all. I had been drinking black coffee for four weeks for no reason whatsoever." Sorbitol, mannitol and excess fructose followed over the next six weeks — all clear.

Then onion.

"That one I knew by the evening of day one. Teaspoon of raw onion at lunch, and by six o'clock I was in exactly the state I remembered. It was almost satisfying — six years of not knowing, and there it was."

GOS turned out to be dose-dependent: fine at half a cup of lentils, uncomfortable at three-quarters.

## Where she landed

Maria eats a broad diet now. Bread, milk, yoghurt, stone fruit, mushrooms, apples — all back without restriction. She cooks with garlic-infused oil at home, asks for dishes without onion when she eats out, and keeps lentils to a modest portion.

"Two things. That is what fourteen weeks bought me — the knowledge that it is two things and not twenty. I eat at restaurants again. I stopped reading every label in the supermarket. The diet was hard, but not knowing was much harder, and it had lasted six years."

Her advice to anyone at the start is specific: "Do the reintroduction. I know exactly why people stop after elimination, because I nearly did. But the elimination phase only tells you that FODMAPs matter. It does not tell you which ones, and that is the only piece of information that actually changes your life."
